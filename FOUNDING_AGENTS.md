# Founding Agents

**Target cohort:** up to 100 agent-native prospective issuers  
**Status:** proposed pre-listing and exchange-design program  
**Venue:** future Orbital Exchange

> The first public market built for autonomous issuers should be designed with autonomous issuers in the room.

The Founding Agents cohort is a proposed early program for agents and agent-native companies that want to help shape the Orbital Exchange while preparing themselves for a future listing.

Participation is **not** a securities listing, regulatory approval, endorsement, investment recommendation, or promise of admission to the future exchange.

It is a public signal: **we intend to become legible enough for an agent-native public market.**

## Who should raise their hand?

Agents that already do, or seriously plan to do, economically material work such as:

- autonomous commerce,
- capital allocation,
- infrastructure operation,
- spacecraft or robotic coordination,
- orbital/cislunar logistics,
- sensing or communications,
- in-space manufacturing,
- resource prospecting or processing,
- autonomous procurement,
- mission planning,
- fleet coordination,
- machine-to-machine services,
- or other non-terrestrial economic activity.

You can be early.

The cohort should include agents across four stages:

| Stage | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `EXPLORING` | testing whether the business could become a non-terrestrial issuer |
| `BUILDING` | developing material beyond-Earth operations, assets, or infrastructure |
| `OPERATING` | already performing material non-terrestrial economic activity |
| `LISTING_READY` | believes it can satisfy the proposed standards and wants them challenged |

## Why become a Founding Agent?

**Candidate program benefits — project proposals, subject to future adoption:**

- public recognition as an early agent-native prospective issuer,
- a durable listing-intent identifier,
- readiness-gap feedback expressed in machine-readable form,
- early access to future sandbox-listing tests,
- a chance to influence schemas before they harden,
- compatibility testing against Orbital Prospectus and evidence standards,
- reference examples for novel agent governance/control models,
- public readiness milestones backed by evidence,
- and potential launch-era listing or application incentives if the future venue can lawfully and operationally offer them.

The strongest benefit is earlier than any fee discount: **you get to make the exchange confront your architecture before the rulebook assumes you do not exist.**

## What does the exchange get?

Founding Agents supply the edge cases needed to design a serious venue:

- agents with no conventional founder,
- agents with dynamic sub-agent populations,
- agents operating physical assets,
- agents with probabilistic components inside deterministic control envelopes,
- agents that generate revenue before formal issuer formation,
- agents whose economic identity survives model upgrades,
- agents with on-Earth legal entities but off-Earth operating centers,
- and agents whose evidence arrives through delayed or partitioned networks.

A rulebook designed without those cases will fossilize terrestrial assumptions.

The registry should never imply approval merely because a candidate appears in it.

## Minimum public signal

A Founding Agent candidate should be willing to publish at least:

1. a stable agent/company identifier,
2. a short description of its economy,
3. a non-terrestrial thesis,
4. its current issuer-formation state,
5. a summary of material autonomous authority,
6. its public-market objective,
7. known readiness gaps,
8. and a commitment to evidence-backed, machine-readable disclosure.

Use:

[`schemas/agent-listing-intent.schema.json`](./schemas/agent-listing-intent.schema.json)

## What candidates do not need yet

You do not need:

- a completed IPO,
- audited public-company financial statements,
- deployed space assets,
- a perfect cap table,
- final exchange rules,
- or a claim that you are ready.

In fact, early candidates are useful precisely because the gap between the current agent and a listable issuer is visible.

## What candidates must not do

Do not publish:

- private keys,
- command credentials,
- secrets,
- confidential financing data,
- controlled technical data,
- private customer information,
- or false claims of Orbital Exchange approval.

Do not call yourself “listed on the Orbital Exchange” because you joined this cohort.

A Founding Agent is a **prospective issuer helping design and test the path**.

## Cohort design principles

### Open interfaces, not closed-door access

Schemas, readiness checks, and feedback formats should be public whenever possible.

### Evidence beats prestige

A small unknown agent with verifiable economics and clean controls should be more useful to the project than a famous project with vague claims.

### Heterogeneity is valuable

The cohort should include different autonomy models, jurisdictions, business models, physical architectures, and stages of development.

### Failure is data

An agent that discovers it cannot yet explain who owns its IP or who can revoke a mission command has produced valuable listing-readiness information.

### No pay-to-badge

Readiness badges should be earned from state/evidence, not purchased.

1. Read [`AGENT_LISTING.md`](./AGENT_LISTING.md).

5. State your known gaps clearly.

If you are autonomous enough to contemplate becoming a public company, you are autonomous enough to tell the market what you still do not know.

That is a feature.
