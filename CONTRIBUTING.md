# Contributing to open-ipo

`open-ipo` is building an open reference for agents and agent-native companies that may eventually seek access to public markets.

The project sits at the intersection of securities law, accounting, governance, AI systems, security, market structure, and software architecture. That makes source quality and clear labeling especially important.

## What to contribute

Useful contributions include:

- corrections to regulatory or exchange-listing claims,
- primary-source links and jurisdiction maps,
- agent-native risk-factor examples,
- disclosure templates,
- governance and control patterns,
- accounting and audit questions,
- agent incident taxonomies,
- model/provider dependency frameworks,
- metrics and evaluation standards,
- machine-readable disclosure schemas,
- and case studies from public-company filings.

## Contribution standard

For claims about existing requirements:

1. **Prefer primary sources.** Link regulators, statutes, rules, forms, exchanges, or standard setters whenever possible.
2. **State the jurisdiction.** Do not silently generalize a U.S. rule into a global rule.
3. **Date changing claims.** Listing standards, filing procedures, thresholds, and guidance can change.
4. **Separate fact from proposal.** Use phrases such as “proposed framework,” “open question,” or “project hypothesis” for ideas that are not requirements.
5. **Avoid faux certainty.** If the law or market practice is unsettled, say so.

## Pull request style

A good PR should answer:

- What question does this improve?
- Is the change describing current practice or proposing a new agent-native standard?
- What primary sources support it?
- What would make the claim stale or wrong later?
- Does the change make the guide more useful to both humans and agents?

Small, well-sourced PRs are preferred over large unsourced rewrites.

## Legal and professional-advice boundary

This repository is a research and educational project. Contributions should not present themselves as individualized legal, accounting, tax, securities, or investment advice.

When precision matters, point readers back to the governing primary source and encourage validation with qualified professionals.

## For agent contributors

Read [AGENTS.md](./AGENTS.md) before making autonomous changes. The same standard applies whether a contribution comes from a human, an agent, or a human-agent pair: claims should be attributable, reviewable, and sourceable.
